RobustConnection promises to restore queues, exchanges, bindings, and consumers after a reconnect, but the current topology bookkeeping loses information and can drift from what was actually acknowledged by the broker. In particular, a binding is identified by more than just its source and routing key: RabbitMQ permits the same key with different argument tables. Mutable argument dictionaries must also be snapshotted when the bind succeeds, otherwise later caller edits silently change the topology that will be replayed.

Make robust topology recovery deterministic and failure-safe for the public RobustExchange and RobustQueue APIs. Successful robust bindings must be retained with their complete identity (source/exchange, destination or queue, routing key, and binding arguments), preserve the arguments as they were at the successful call, and replay each distinct binding once after the object is redeclared. Re-registering an identical binding should not create duplicate replay calls, while a different argument table remains a separate binding. Replay must perform declaration first, then all saved bindings in registration order, and only then restore robust consumers in registration order.

Unbinding must remove only the exact binding requested, and its saved record is removed only after the broker unbind call succeeds; if that call raises, the binding remains eligible for a later reconnect. The same success-before-removal rule applies to RobustQueue.cancel: a failed cancellation must leave that consumer saved for replay, while a successful cancellation removes it. Bindings and consumers created with robust=False are intentionally ephemeral and must never be replayed. Preserve existing method signatures, return values, and ordinary (non-robust) AMQP operations.
